How the model is working now:
- The deployed backend uses a trained model on 215 acoustic features.
- `jitter`, `shimmer`, `hnr`, and `f0_mean` are part of the extracted feature set, but they are not all hard-coded as direct pass/fail rules.
- `f0_mean` is not used as a decision threshold in the backend logic. It is displayed, but the code does not classify based on a specific `f0_mean` cutoff.

The actual hard thresholds in main.py are:
- Compression / very low confidence:
  - `jitter > 0.08` and `shimmer > 0.35`, or
  - `jitter > 0.06` and `shimmer > 0.40`, or
  - `hnr < 5`
- Strong PD-like confidence:
  - `jitter > 0.035` or `shimmer > 0.30`
  - `jitter > 0.028` and `shimmer > 0.22`
  - `jitter > 0.025` and `shimmer > 0.20`
  - `jitter > 0.020` and `shimmer > 0.15`
- Healthy-leaning signal:
  - `jitter < 0.008` and `shimmer < 0.08` and `hnr > 20`

For the fallback scorer, the boundary is basically:
- Start at `score = 50`
- Convert to probability with `pd_pred = score / 100`
- So the practical class boundary is around `50%`
- Then risk bands are:
  - `< 15` very low
  - `< 30` low
  - `< 50` moderate
  - `< 65` elevated
  - `< 80` high
  - `>= 80` very high

So the short version is: there is no fixed `f0_mean` cutoff, and the real class boundary is the model’s learned probability, with `50%` as the main healthy-vs-PD split in the fallback logic.

Use this wording in your project:

Your app works as a voice-based screening pipeline. A user uploads or records speech in index.html, the browser sends the audio to the FastAPI backend in main.py, and the backend extracts acoustic features from the signal using feature_utils.py. Those features are scaled and passed into the trained TensorFlow model, which outputs a Parkinson’s probability. The backend then turns that probability into a risk label such as Very Low, Low, Moderate, High, or Very High.

What ML model is used depends on which part of the repo you mean:
- The current deployed backend uses a unified Keras/TensorFlow neural network loaded from `unified_advanced_pd_fixed.keras` or the fallback `unified_advanced_pd.keras`.
- The training scripts also show ensemble experiments with a neural network plus XGBoost, and in some versions Random Forest/other classifiers.
- So for the live app, the main model is the neural network, not a multi-model ensemble.

The important feature terms mean:
- Jitter: tiny cycle-to-cycle pitch instability. Higher values can indicate unstable vocal folds and are often associated with Parkinsonian speech.
- Shimmer: tiny cycle-to-cycle loudness/amplitude instability. Higher values mean the voice amplitude varies more from one cycle to the next.
- HNR: harmonic-to-noise ratio. Higher HNR means a cleaner, more periodic voice; lower HNR means more noise/breathiness.
- F0 mean: average fundamental frequency, basically the average pitch of the voice.
- Spectral centroid: where the “center of mass” of the sound spectrum sits; higher values usually mean a brighter voice.
- Zero crossing rate: how often the waveform crosses zero; higher values often reflect noisier or more unvoiced sound.
- MFCCs: compact numerical summaries of voice timbre and texture; the model uses them because they capture patterns humans do not easily hear directly.

The backend does not use a single fixed cutoff for all features. The real classification boundary is the model’s predicted probability, with these display bands in main.py:
- below 15%: Very Low Risk
- below 30%: Low Risk
- below 50%: Moderate Risk
- below 65%: Elevated Risk
- below 80%: High Risk
- 80% and above: Very High Risk

The fallback scoring path in main.py uses rough heuristic thresholds like:
- HNR > 18 lowers risk
- HNR < 15 raises risk
- Jitter > 0.008 raises risk
- Shimmer > 0.20 raises risk
